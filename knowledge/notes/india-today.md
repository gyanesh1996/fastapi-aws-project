# India today: why lifestyle change matters

## india-diet-disease-burden
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Introduction, PDF p. 15
Estimates show that 56.4% of total disease burden in India is due to unhealthy diets. Healthy diets and physical activity can reduce a substantial proportion of coronary heart disease (CHD) and hypertension (HTN) and prevent upto 80% of type 2 diabetes. A significant proportion of premature deaths can be averted by following a healthy lifestyle.

## india-adults-nfhs5
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Table II (data from NFHS 5, 2021), PDF p. 16, transcribed from a table
Among adults aged 18–69 years in India as per NFHS 5 (2021): Diabetes (Type 2) men 15.6%, women 13.5%. Hypertension men 24.0%, women 21.3%. Overweight/obesity men 22.9%, women 24.0%. Abdominal obesity men 47.7%, women 56.7%.

## india-too-much-cereal
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Introduction, PDF p. 16
However, as per the data, cereals contribute to 50% to 70% of total energy per day. Pulses, meat, poultry and fish together contribute to 6% to 9% of the total energy per day as against the recommended intake level of 14% of total energy from these foods.

## india-refined-over-whole
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Introduction, PDF p. 16
In a large segment of the country's population, the intake of micronutrient-dense foods (whole grains, pulses, beans, nuts, fresh vegetables, fruits, etc.) is found to be lower than the recommend levels, whereas, the intake of refined cereals is found to be higher.

## india-junk-food-marketing
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Introduction, PDF p. 15
Research indicates that unhealthy, highly processed, high-fat, sugar and salt (HFSS) foods have become more affordable and accessible than the healthier alternatives. Aggressive advertising and marketing of these unhealthy foods through different media channels, including social media, are seen to influence dietary preferences among both children and adults.
