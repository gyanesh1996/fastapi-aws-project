# Oils and fats

## oil-limit-per-day
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 1, PDF p. 18
Consuming adequate quantities of nuts, oilseeds, fatty fish and restricting cooking oils to 25g to 30g per day.

## oil-vanaspati
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 1, PDF p. 27
It is important to limit intake of cooking oils (vegetable oils), saturated fat (butter, ghee) and avoid partially hydrogenated vegetable oils (vanaspathi).

## oil-points-to-register
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 7 "Points to register", PDF p. 73
Limit the use of high saturated-fat containing oils/fats such as ghee, butter etc. Minimize consumption of ready-to-eat fast foods, bakery foods and processed foods which may contain trans fats and saturated fats. Avoid using partially hydrogenated fats (vanaspati) as the cooking medium as they may contain trans-fats and saturated fats.

## oil-no-reheating
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 7 "Points to register", PDF p. 73
Repeated heating of cooking oils generates harmful oxidative (polar) compounds and hence must be avoided.

## oil-omega3-foods
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 7 "Points to register", PDF p. 73
Include foods rich in alpha-linolenic (ALA)/n-3 PUFA such as nuts & seeds, soyabeans, grains/millets, green leafy vegetables and fenugreek seeds.

## who-trans-fat
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
In particular, industrially-produced trans fats are not part of a healthy diet and should be avoided.

## who-reduce-fat-cooking
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
Fat intake, especially saturated and trans fat, can be reduced by: steaming or boiling instead of frying when cooking.
