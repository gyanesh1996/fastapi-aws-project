# Vegetables and fruits

## veg-points-to-register
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 6 "Points to register", PDF p. 67
To achieve consumption of atleast 400g of vegetables per day, include fresh vegetables and green leafy vegetables in every meal. Eat a variety of vegetables and fruits in all your meals in various forms (raw salad, curry, dry cooked vegetables, soups, as raita mixed with curd or added to pulse and cereal preparations). Prefer root vegetables to tubers.

## veg-iron-vitamin-c
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 6, PDF p. 65
Iron is available in plenty in green leafy vegetables, but the absorption is limited. Vitamin C rich foods like guava or orange must be consumed after meals to improve iron absorption.

## veg-vitamin-a-colours
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 6, PDF p. 65
Carotenoids (precursors of vitamin A) are plentiful in fruits and vegetables that are green or deep yellow / orange in colour, such as green leafy vegetables, carrots, tomatoes, capsicum, orange-flesh, sweet potatoes, papaya, mango, etc.

## fruit-not-juice
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 14 "Points to register", PDF p. 109
Consume fresh fruits rather than in juice form.

## who-fruit-veg-400g
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
Everyone older than 10 years of age should aim for at least 400 grams of fruits and vegetables per day.

## who-fruit-juice-sugar
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
While fruit juice can be consumed, most varieties including those without added sugars contain significant amounts of free sugars, the consumption of which should be limited.
