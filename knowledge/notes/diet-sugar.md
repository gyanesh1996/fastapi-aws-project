# Sugar, jaggery, honey, sweeteners

## sugar-high-definition
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 15, PDF p. 111
Consumption of sugar in quantities that contribute over 5% of total energy intake per day or 25 g/day (based on average intake of 2000 Kcal/day) is defined as 'high' sugar. Limiting sugar to 25g/day is better for health. If possible, added sugar may be completely eliminated from one's diet as it adds no nutritive value other than calories.

## sugar-jaggery-honey-are-added-sugar
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 15, PDF p. 111
Added sugars refer to sugars and sugar syrups added to foods and drinks during processing and preparation and they include sucrose (table sugar), jaggery, honey, glucose, fructose, dextrose etc. Adding sugar over and above what is naturally/ inherently present in foods increases the total calorie intake, but adds no nutritive value.

## sugar-substitutes
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 15, PDF p. 111
Studies have indicated that long term consumption of non-calorie sugar substitutes could lead to overweight/obesity, diabetes, hypertension and other NCDs. Studies also indicate disruption in beneficial intestinal bacterial flora. Hence, pregnant and lactating women and children should avoid sugar substitutes.

## sugar-avoid-added
source: ICMR-NIN Dietary Guidelines for Indians 2024, Guideline 1, PDF p. 19
Limit intake of foods with added fat/oil and salt. Avoid foods and beverages with added sugars.

## who-free-sugars-limit
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
The consumption of free sugars should be limited to less than 10% of total daily energy intake, which is equivalent to 50 g (or about 12 level teaspoons) for a person of healthy body weight consuming about 2000 calories per day. Limiting further to 5% or less of total daily energy intake may provide additional health benefits.

## who-free-sugars-include-honey
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
Free sugars include monosaccharides and disaccharides (e.g. sucrose, fructose, glucose) added to foods and beverages by the manufacturer, cook or consumer, and sugars naturally present in honey, syrups, fruit juices and fruit juice concentrates.

## who-no-sweeteners
source: WHO fact sheet "Healthy diet"
Reducing consumption of free sugars (and maintaining low levels of intake) should be accomplished without the use of non-sugar sweeteners.
